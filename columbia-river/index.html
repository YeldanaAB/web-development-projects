About the Columbia River

The Columbia River is a major river in the Pacific Northwest. It starts in British Columbia, Canada, and flows through Washington and along the border between Washington and Oregon. It eventually reaches the Pacific Ocean. The river is about 1,243 miles long and has several major tributaries, including the Snake, Kootenay, Spokane, Yakima, and Willamette Rivers.

Importance of the Columbia River

For thousands of years, the Columbia River has been an important part of life in the Pacific Northwest. Indigenous peoples have relied on the river and its salmon for food and transportation. Today, it is used for transportation, fishing, irrigation, and hydroelectric power. Dams along the river help produce electricity and support navigation and irrigation.
